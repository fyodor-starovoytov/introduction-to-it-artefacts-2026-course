6 · The loudspeaker —

Numbers become voltage becomes moving air. Turn the speaker on (headphones!) and you hear every knob above. If adding bits stops improving this number, the bottleneck has moved: it is the sampling rate throwing away the high harmonics, not the quantizer. Raise that instead — and you have just re-derived why the telephone sounds thin.

How to drive it in a lecture. Start in test tone at 1200 Hz with the sampling rate at 8000 Hz — everything behaves. Now drag the tone slider up past 4000 Hz and watch stage 2: the dots start tracing a slower wave than the input, and the red banner tells you what frequency the recording now claims to be. Nothing downstream can ever know. Then set the anti-alias filter to ON/OFF and watch the difference land before the sampler.

Next: drop bits to 3 and watch stage 3 turn into a staircase (turn the speaker on to hear the hiss appear). Then raise channel noise and compare the two lanes in stage 5 — the analog copy degrades smoothly, the digital one is perfect, perfect, perfect… then falls off a cliff when the noise is big enough to flip a bit.
